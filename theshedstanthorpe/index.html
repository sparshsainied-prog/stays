SHED

Matthew Street, Stanthorpe, in the Granite Belt

An architect-designed shed house for six, in the middle of town.

Two queen rooms, a bunk nook and a fire that warms the whole house. High Street is under five minutes away on foot.

Come in out of the cold.

Firewood is supplied. The table seats six, and the ceiling goes up to the pitch of the roof.

Built like a shearing shed

Karlie is an architect. She designed The Shed after the old Queensland shearing sheds and the working sheds around town: black Colorbond on the outside, a bright open room with a steep pitched ceiling inside.

After ten years of staying in other people's Airbnbs, she built the one she wanted to stay in.

Granite in the floor

Local granite stones show through the polished concrete floor.

The wood heater warms the whole house on winter nights, and the firewood is already there. The Shed is air conditioned and heated as well.

Solar on the roof. Solar panels on site, a water tank for the garden and refillable toiletries.

All on one level. One small step at the front door, wide halls, lever handles and a step-free shower.

Room for six

Two queen bedrooms and a bunk nook off the hall, around one open living, dining and kitchen room. Queensland artists' work hangs on the walls.

Leave the car in the drive

The Shed is on a quiet street in the middle of Stanthorpe. Restaurants, pubs and cafes are a walk away, and so is Quart Pot Creek, where the big thermometer gives the morning temperature.
