The Farmhouse at Birkenburn Farm

Five bedrooms, room for 16, and only one group at a time, on a 2700 acre property farmed by the same family since the 1890s.

After dark, the firepit terrace sits between the main house, the Studio and the Bunkhouse. Wood for the open fire inside is supplied.

Five rooms, one group

Most of the day happens outside

A garden detailed by a landscape architect and fenced all round, so small children and dogs stay safe. The table under the wisteria seats 16.

Who lives here

More than 60 bird species have been counted on the farm so far. Bring binoculars for the early morning and late afternoon walks.

In October the farm took water samples from its stretch of Sandhills Creek with the Mulloon Institute. The eDNA in them will show which species live in the creek, down to the microscopic ones.

  • GalahsOne of the more obvious of the 60.
  • KookaburrasYou'll hear them from bed.
  • Flame robinsListed as vulnerable. Look in the woodland.
  • EchidnasShy, and hunting ants.
  • WombatsBurrows in most parts of the farm.
  • KangaroosWith wallabies, possums and the black Angus cows.

How far things are

Close to Canberra, Braidwood and Bungendore for wineries, galleries and restaurants. Everything below is on the farm.

  1. 6.5 kmof dirt road from the Kings Highway to the door. Fine in a 2wd.
  2. 3 minwalk to the Camp Kitchen, a covered firepit and picnic shelter.
  3. 10 minwalk to the jetty and the dam.
  4. 1.5, 2.5, 4 kmmarked walking trails, along Sandhills Creek and up to Twynam trig.
  5. 2700acres in all, with 900 protected under conservation agreements.

Tom and Martina

Tom grew up here. His family has farmed Birkenburn since the 1890s, and he and Martina have lived and worked on it together since 1998.

Their backgrounds are in architecture and landscape design. They're turning a sheep and cattle farm into one with conservation work, workshops and places to stay, and are part of the group of landholders forming the Mulloon Rehydration Initiative.
