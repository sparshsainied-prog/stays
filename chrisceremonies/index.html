Why can't a ceremony be fun?

Born and raised in Sydney, now in Melbourne, Chris kept leaving friends' weddings asking why the ceremony couldn't feel as exciting as everyone in the room.

His answer is that it can. Ceremonies can be fun, exhilarating and joyous, while you look back on the moments that brought the two of you together.

He has years of hosting events of all shapes and sizes, and a couple of Melbourne International Comedy Festival shows behind him. So he can be your celebrant, your MC, or both.

Pick your ceremony

  1. Fully Customised Ceremony

    The whole shebang, 15 to 30 minutes.

    • Your story retold: how you met, first dates, the engagement, pets, what makes you you. Full of laughs and maybe a couple of tears.
    • Introduction, acknowledgments, readings, parental blessing, a special ceremony, I do's, legal and personal vows, rings
    • All your music before, during and after
    • Your official marriage certificate from Births, Deaths and Marriages, bought and organised for you

  2. The Joey Tribbiani

    A friend or family member marries you.

    • A full ceremony script for your friend to make their own
    • Chris looks after every legal part, the paperwork and the music
    • Your friend comes to the first meeting, and usually gets an online catch-up with Chris before the day

  3. Just the Basics

    Skip the waffling and get to the party. 5 to 10 minutes.

    • A brief introduction, I do's, legal vows, personal vows and rings
    • All your music, and your official certificate

  4. MC the Reception

    Keep your guests laughing and the night on time.

    • Chris runs the run sheet and keeps your vendors and speech makers up to date as things change

Questions couples ask

Who plays the music when we walk down the aisle? Chris does.

He plays your music before, during and after the ceremony, out of his own speaker. In his words, many celebrants "think 'Spotify' is for stain removal".

What happens between booking and the day?

You meet about six months out to sign and lodge the Notice of Intended Marriage and talk through each part of the ceremony. Then two questionnaires: one about wording and songs, one about your story. Chris writes the ceremony from those, and checks in when something needs ironing out. Most couples need one meeting and a few emails.

Can we read it first?

You're welcome to. Chris's advice is that you'll have a better time if you don't. You'll have planned every other part of the day, so a surprise is nice.
